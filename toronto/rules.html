Toronto Poker Club League rules · this season

How the League Works

The short version, before the details.

📅

Seasonal play

"Regulars" are players that commit to a full season of 10 games. Each game is a No-Limit Hold’em tournament with a small prize for the top finishers that night.

💵

Season cost

Your spot costs $250 for the season — a $25 buy-in for each of the 10 games. You owe the full amount even if you miss some games, because the season’s prize money depends on everyone’s share.

🤝

Money changes hands once

We keep track of who owes what all season and settle up once, after the Final Game.

🏆

Most of the money goes to the Final Game

$15 of every buy-in is set aside for the Final Game at the end of the season. By then the pot is $1,050 — far more than any single night — and it’s paid to the top three: $600, $300 and $150.

📈

Regular games earn you a head start

Everyone starts the Final Game with 25,000 in chips. Finishing 1st, 2nd or 3rd in a regular game adds 4,000, 2,000 or 1,000 bonus chips to your Final Game stack, so playing well all season makes you harder to knock out when it counts.

👥

Guests are welcome

Guests are welcome; they put $10 into that night’s pot and aren’t part of the season.

Rule Details

The Game

  • No-Limit Texas Hold’em — rotating dealer
  • 7 regular players, 10 sessions this season
  • Games are held in the Toronto area
  • Must have 3 or more regular players to play a game
  • House rule: don’t deal cards to players not at the table
  • We lean forgiving on rules for less experienced players — calls are collectively decided (the Final Game can be stricter)

Buy-In & Cost

  • $25 buy-in — $10 to the weekly pot, $15 to the Final Game pot
  • Total season cost: $250
  • Guests contribute only $10, toward the weekly pot

Attendance & Timing

  • Try to arrive by 7:15pm, cards fly at 7:30pm
  • If you don’t tell us you’re coming and aren’t there by the end of Round 1, we pull your chips
  • You can be late to a game as long as you let the group know you are planning to come. However, you cannot place in the top three without being at the table
  • If a week has a lot of scheduling conflicts, we can collectively move the game — if no agreement is reached, it goes on as planned
